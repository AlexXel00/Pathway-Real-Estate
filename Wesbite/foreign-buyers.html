Foreign Buyers
Made Clear

A practical overview of the most common and realistic ways foreigners can secure property in the Philippines, from simple condominium ownership to long-term land structures.

If you are buying an apartment or condominium unit without land ownership, the process is generally straightforward. Foreign nationals may legally acquire condominium units, provided the legal foreign ownership limit within the condominium project is respected. In other words: buying a condo is usually not a problem for foreign buyers.

The main restrictions apply to land ownership. For foreign buyers who want to secure land, there are several possible structures. Some are strong and widely used. Others may look simple at first, but carry serious legal risk.

Below is a practical overview of the most common options, what they mean, what they typically involve, and how we assess them based on real-world experience in the Philippines.

Your Main Options

Different buyers need different structures. The right solution depends on whether your goal is a private home, a lifestyle property, an investment, or a business project.

01
Direct ownership

Condominiums
& Apartments

The simplest option for foreign buyers

Buyers looking for the easiest and most direct purchase route
Low

Foreigners can generally buy condominium or apartment units without difficulty, as long as the legal foreign ownership cap in the condominium project is respected.

This makes condos the most direct and uncomplicated property route for international buyers who do not need land title in their own structure.

If your goal is a home, holiday unit, rental investment, or low-complexity entry into the Philippine property market, this is usually the cleanest starting point.

Our view

If your goal is simplicity and direct acquisition, this is the easiest legal option available to foreign nationals.

02
Land ownership structure

Buying Land Through
a Philippine Corporation

The most common and most established legal route for foreign land buyers

Usually around 3 to 6 weeks, often up to one month or slightly more
Approx. PHP 130,000 plus value-based and annual compliance costs

This is the structure most serious foreign buyers and many larger investors use when they want to acquire land in the Philippines in a legally recognized way. Instead of buying the land in your personal name, the land is acquired through a local Philippine corporation.

As a foreign buyer, your first step is to identify suitable Filipino shareholders or members for the local structure. After that, a local lawyer prepares the corporate documents, the company is registered, the local tax process is completed, and a local bank account is opened so the company can acquire the land.

As a practical rule of thumb, many buyers should expect around PHP 130,000 for setup, legal work, and incorporation-related costs, plus roughly 1% of the property value for the BIR-related payment in many practical cases, as well as possible annual accounting, compliance, and structure-related costs depending on how the company is set up and used.

It is highly advisable to have a Philippine lawyer prepare the internal agreements very carefully. Governance, voting thresholds, funding rights, reserved matters, exits, and internal protections should all be documented properly. This is one of the strongest available solutions, but only if it is set up correctly.

Our view

For buyers who want to hold land in a serious, scalable, and legally structured way, this is often the best overall option.

03
Our company offer

Long-Term
Lease

A highly attractive and cost-efficient alternative for foreign buyers

50 years total, based on two 25-year contract periods
Often around 5 to 10% lower total entry burden

Leasing is often the most affordable land solution for foreign buyers, because transfer-related costs and several other acquisition expenses can be reduced. In many practical cases, the overall price burden may be around 5 to 10% lower than in a more complex acquisition structure.

How our model works: You choose the property you want. You pay for it, and we acquire it for you. The property is then leased to you under a 50-year structure consisting of two 25-year contract periods.

During this time, you receive the contractual rights and control over the property, together with the obligations attached to it, such as land taxes and related responsibilities. Whether your goal is private construction or a business project, the structure is designed to give you long-term use and security.

This is a legal and secure option when documented correctly. From our side, the lease cannot be arbitrarily terminated, provided the agreed contract terms are respected. The second contract period is secured through the lease structure we offer.

From the end of the first contract period and until one year before the end of the second period, you may have the option to convert the arrangement into a purchase if your legal situation has changed and a lawful transfer structure is available to you at that time.

If you later decide to buy the property, you do not pay for the land a second time. Under our standard model, the conversion is usually based only on a fee of 15% of the current land value at the time of transfer, plus the applicable transfer fees and legal fees.

What about the new 99-year lease law? In 2025 the Philippines passed RA 12252, which allows foreign investors to lease private land for up to 99 years. It is aimed at registered investment projects: for tourism, for example, it only applies to projects with a minimum investment of USD 5 million. For most private buyers, the standard long-term lease remains the relevant route.

We deliberately offer 50 years (two 25-year terms). It is a term we can clearly document, stand behind and service ourselves, rather than promising conditions nobody can guarantee a century from now.

Our view

For many foreign buyers, this is an excellent legal and practical solution: secure, attractive, and cost-efficient.

04
Not recommended

Buying Through
a Filipino Nominee

Simple on paper, but not something we recommend

Simple, low-cost, and fast compared with formal structures
High

This is the easiest-looking option and often the cheapest at first glance: a Filipino friend, partner, or nominee buys the land in their own name, while the foreign buyer finances the deal and may hold side documents.

The problem is simple: the foreign buyer does not hold legal title. Even if side agreements exist, the structure is very weak in a dispute because the foreign party is trying to rely on a setup that can conflict with the legal rules on land ownership.

It may look easy in the beginning, but if anything goes wrong later, the foreign buyer is in a much weaker position.

Our view

We do not recommend this option. For buyers who want a serious, stable, and defensible long-term structure, there are better paths.
